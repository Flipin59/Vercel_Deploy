# Financial Literacy: 60 Questions — Rerated on a 1–5 Scale

**Audience:** Classes 11–12  |  **Format:** Single-answer MCQs  |  **Total:** 60 questions  |  **Rating scale:** 1–5

Choose exactly one option per question. Every question has topic tags and a difficulty rating. Answers and explanations follow all 60 questions.

**Revised difficulty:** All 60 questions are rated individually from 1/5 (Easy) to 5/5 (Stretch), for students in Classes 11–12.

All numerical scenarios are hypothetical. Apply the assumptions in each question. Tax examples are simplified exercises, not statements of current Indian tax law. A basic calculator may be used.

An attacker can control a lookalike domain and send authenticated mail from it. Branding does not establish ownership either. A missing app alert is a reason to check, not proof that no genuine update exists. Independent verification resolves the issue without trusting the email's link.

**Related reading:** [FTC: Protect yourself from phishing scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/04/protect-yourself-phishing-scams)

**Q34 — A. The observed payouts can be funded entirely by new members' money; payment to early members does not verify the claimed investment profits, and the pattern is a serious Ponzi warning.**

New contributions of ₹1,00,000 cover ₹60,000 of payouts plus ₹10,000 of costs, leaving ₹30,000. None of those cash flows demonstrates investment earnings. Funding old investors with new investors' contributions is a Ponzi warning, but this month's records cannot establish an exact collapse date or each person's eventual loss.

**Related reading:** [Ponzi scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponzi_scheme)

**Q35 — C. Do not pay more; the earlier ₹800 does not make the new payment more credible. Preserve the messages and payment record, then report through official channels.**

The ₹800 is already spent; paying more does not make the promised prize or refund reliable. Repeated advance fees and pressure to recover an earlier payment are warning signs. Records help an official report, while another promise from the same sender is not independent confirmation.

**Related reading:** [Advance-fee scam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advance-fee_scam); [FTC: What to do if you were scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed); [Opportunity cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_cost)

**Q36 — C. Secure the reused email password and recovery settings first, then replace other reused passwords and check account activity; keep two-factor authentication enabled.**

The compromised password may expose the email account, which is also a recovery route into other accounts. Securing that account and replacing reused passwords addresses the connected risk. A unique financial-account password and two-factor authentication help, but they do not justify leaving the recovery email exposed.

**Related reading:** [FTC: Protect your personal information](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/protect-your-personal-information-hackers-and-scammers?os=firetv)

**Q37 — D. Report it promptly through official bank channels, secure the affected access and preserve transaction details**

Prompt reporting and securing the affected access can limit further loss. Preserve evidence and use official channels; reporting does not guarantee recovery.

**Related reading:** [NPCI: Register a complaint](https://www.npci.org.in/register-a-complaint); [FTC: What to do if you were scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)

**Q38 — C. Independently find the lender's official contact, check whether the loan exists and dispute it if unauthorised; preserve the notice and secure any affected accounts.**

There could be identity misuse, a false notice or another error. Correct personal details do not settle which explanation is true. An independently verified contact lets you investigate and dispute a real unauthorised account without first paying or disclosing more information to an unverified sender.

**Related reading:** [Identity theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_theft); [FTC: Protect yourself from phishing scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/04/protect-yourself-phishing-scams); [FTC: What to do if you were scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)

**Q39 — A. The connection is encrypted, but the operator could still be a scammer**

HTTPS protects data in transit to that website. A scammer can also operate an HTTPS website, so encryption alone does not prove legitimacy.

**Related reading:** [FTC: Encryption and website safety](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/are-public-wi-fi-networks-safe-what-you-need-know)

**Q40 — B. Contact the genuine firm using details independently obtained from the regulator's listing**

Scammers can copy a real firm's name and registration number. Independent contact helps establish whether you are dealing with the genuine firm, not a clone.

**Related reading:** [FCA: Clone firms and individuals](https://www.fca.org.uk/consumers/clone-firms-individuals)

### Investment products, strategies & tax

**Q41 — D. ₹20; the fund issues 500 new units, so net assets and units rise proportionately.**

Initial NAV is (₹5,20,000 − ₹20,000) ÷ 25,000 = ₹20. The ₹10,000 contribution buys 500 units. Net assets become ₹5,10,000 and units become 25,500, so NAV remains ₹20. A contribution at NAV does not itself create an investment gain.

**Related reading:** [Mutual fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_fund)

**Q42 — B. The ETF purchase costs ₹150 more, and its purchase price is 2% above its stated NAV.**

Buying the ETF at the ask costs 100 × ₹102 = ₹10,200. The mutual-fund purchase costs 100 × ₹100.50 = ₹10,050, a ₹150 difference. The ETF price premium is (₹102 − ₹100) ÷ ₹100 = 2%. A premium is not a promise of extra return.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: ETFs](https://investor.vanguard.com/investment-products/etfs); [Mutual fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_fund)

**Q43 — B. 76%, because both the fund's underlying stocks and the separate stock holding count.**

The fund contributes ₹12,000 × 60% = ₹7,200 in stock exposure. Adding ₹8,000 of direct stocks gives ₹15,200 out of a ₹20,000 portfolio, or 76%. An all-in-one fund's asset mix describes that fund, not automatically every investment its owner holds.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: All-in-one funds](https://investor.vanguard.com/investment-products/mutual-funds/all-in-one-funds)

**Q44 — C. It aims to cover a broad domestic stock market, while still carrying stock-market risk**

Total-market coverage is broad within the specified market. The label does not mean every asset class, every country, or freedom from market losses.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: Total-market ETF portfolios](https://investor.vanguard.com/investment-products/etfs/etf-investment-options); [Investor.gov: Diversify your investments](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/save-and-invest/diversify-your-investments)

**Q45 — B. A accepts only Y; B accepts only X.**

A excludes X and Z because they produce fossil fuels, then accepts Y's score of 75. B accepts X's score of 92, while Y and Z fall below its threshold of 80. The ESG label alone does not tell you which exclusions or scoring rules a fund applies.

**Related reading:** [Environmental, social and governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental,_social,_and_governance)

**Q46 — B. The account grows to ₹20,500, but it is ₹500 short of buying the same basket.**

Interest adds ₹600 and the fee removes ₹100, leaving ₹20,500. The basket now costs ₹20,000 × 1.05 = ₹21,000. The nominal balance has risen, but it buys less of that basket and is ₹500 short of buying all of it. Inflation affects what money can buy; it is not a withdrawal from the account.

**Related reading:** [Cash and cash equivalents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_and_cash_equivalents); [Inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation)

**Q47 — A. The fund ends at ₹10,434 and the benchmark at ₹10,560; the fund trails by ₹126 despite leading after year 1.**

The fund's net value is ₹10,000 × 1.11 × 0.94 = ₹10,434. The benchmark reaches ₹10,000 × 1.10 × 0.96 = ₹10,560. The fund trails by ₹126. Active management seeks an outcome but does not guarantee outperformance; costs and the whole comparison period matter.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: Index funds vs. actively managed funds](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/understanding-investment-types/index-funds-vs-actively-managed-funds)

**Q48 — C. −8.5%; the fee makes the fund's return 0.5 percentage points below the index's −8%.**

The index falls 8%, so the fund's value before its fee is ₹9,200. Deducting ₹50 leaves ₹9,150, a loss of ₹850 on ₹10,000, or 8.5%. The difference is 0.5 percentage points. Tracking an index does not remove its losses or the effect of fund costs.

**Related reading:** [Index fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_fund)

**Q49 — C. They differ in product type but share an index style and stock exposure**

ETF and mutual fund describe product structures. Both can follow an index and hold the same asset class, so the labels describe separate dimensions.

**Related reading:** [Mutual fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_fund); [Vanguard: ETFs](https://investor.vanguard.com/investment-products/etfs); [Index fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_fund)

**Q50 — D. Your voting share falls from 4% to 3.2%; the counts alone do not determine the change in market value.**

Before issuance, you own 40 ÷ 1,000 = 4% of the votes; afterwards, 40 ÷ 1,250 = 3.2%. The share issuance dilutes your voting percentage. Its effect on market value also depends on the funds raised, issue terms and the company's prospects, so the count alone cannot establish that effect.

**Related reading:** [Stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock)

**Q51 — B. The coupon terms can be fixed while the bond's market price changes**

Fixed coupon terms do not fix a bond's market price. Market interest rates and credit conditions can affect its price, and payments still depend on the issuer meeting its obligations.

**Related reading:** [Fixed income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_income)

**Q52 — D. The bill followed by cash; it covers the deadline without relying on the ten-year bond's resale price.**

The 30-day bill matures before the payment and its proceeds can be held for 15 days. The ten-year bond would need to be sold well before maturity, making the available amount depend on its market price. Credit quality and maturity matching address different risks. Short-term debt instruments are central to money markets.

**Related reading:** [Money market fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_market_fund)

**Q53 — D. Exact restoration is impossible without sales: even putting all ₹1,000 into stocks leaves a ₹200 shortfall against the target stock holding.**

After returns, stocks are ₹6,000 × 0.80 = ₹4,800 and bonds are ₹4,000 × 1.05 = ₹4,200. Including the deposit, total value is ₹10,000 and the target stock holding is ₹6,000. Stocks need ₹1,200, more than the ₹1,000 deposit. Putting all new cash into stocks leaves ₹5,800, so reaching exactly 60% requires a ₹200 bond-to-stock transfer. The stated no-sales constraint makes exact restoration impossible.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: Multi-asset and balanced funds](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/mutual-funds/what-are-multi-asset-balanced-funds)

**Q54 — A. A remains concentrated in one sector despite having the same number of holdings**

Diversification depends on the exposures, not just the count of holdings. Technology companies can be affected together by sector-specific developments.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: Sector and specialty funds](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/understanding-investment-types/what-are-sector-specialty-funds); [Investor.gov: Diversify your investments](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/save-and-invest/diversify-your-investments)

**Q55 — D. B ends with ₹11,680, which is ₹16 more than A's ₹11,664.**

A keeps an 8% annual return after interest tax, reaching ₹10,000 × 1.08² = ₹11,664. B reaches ₹12,100 before sale; tax is 20% of its ₹2,100 gain, or ₹420, leaving ₹11,680. Deferring the tax allows more money to compound, producing a ₹16 advantage under the stated assumptions.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: Tax-efficient investments](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/taxes/tax-saving-investments)

**Q56 — B. The immediate tax saving less transaction cost is ₹190; the lifetime after-tax benefit still depends on later gains and tax outcomes.**

Tax before the action is 20% × ₹3,000 = ₹600. After the offset it is 20% × (₹3,000 − ₹1,200) = ₹360, saving ₹240 now. Subtracting ₹50 in transaction costs leaves ₹190. The action does not erase every future tax liability; later gains and tax treatment affect the lifetime result.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: Tax-efficient investments](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/taxes/tax-saving-investments)

**Q57 — D. ₹300**

The exemption applies to the ₹500 interest only. The ₹300 sale profit remains taxable. Always distinguish which income or tax an exemption covers.

**Related reading:** [Tax exemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_exemption); [Vanguard: Tax-efficient investments](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/taxes/tax-saving-investments)

**Q58 — B. ₹1,200**

Stocks rise from ₹6,000 to ₹9,000; bonds remain ₹4,000. Total = ₹13,000. Target stocks = 60% × ₹13,000 = ₹7,800, so move ₹1,200 from stocks to bonds.

**Related reading:** [Asset allocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_allocation); [Vanguard: Multi-asset and balanced funds](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/mutual-funds/what-are-multi-asset-balanced-funds)

**Q59 — C. B, by 0.3 percentage points**

A's after-tax return = 6% × (1 − 0.25) = 4.5%. B retains 4.8%. B is higher by 0.3 percentage points under the stated hypothetical rules.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: Tax-efficient investments](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/taxes/tax-saving-investments)

**Q60 — A. The active fund, by ₹37.50**

Active: ₹1,000 gain − ₹200 fee = ₹800; after 25% tax, ₹600 remains. Index: ₹800 − ₹50 = ₹750; after tax, ₹562.50 remains. Final balances are ₹10,600 and ₹10,562.50, a ₹37.50 difference. These are realised hypothetical outcomes, not forecasts.

**Related reading:** [Vanguard: Index funds vs. actively managed funds](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/understanding-investment-types/index-funds-vs-actively-managed-funds); [Investor.gov: Understanding fees](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/understanding-fees); [Vanguard: Tax-efficient investments](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/taxes/tax-saving-investments)
