Module 1: Plan & Budget (Peter & Stewie)
Reel 1: Needs vs Wants
#NeedVsWant #PocketMoney #Budgeting #ImpulseBuying #FinancialLiteracy #StudentLife #MoneyBrainrot
Peter: Hey Stewie, I just got two thousand rupees for the whole month. I'm buying wireless earphones, a new game skin, and biryani for the boys every Friday. Hehehehe.
Stewie: Peter, you absolute walnut. What about your bus pass? Your phone recharge? Your exam fee? Your books?
Peter: Those sound like future Peter's problems.
Stewie: Future Peter is walking to school, with no phone, and a failed exam. Listen carefully. A need is something you cannot survive the month without. A want is something that's just nice to have.
Peter: Biryani is a need, Stewie. I would literally die without it.
Stewie: Food is a need. Biryani with the boys every Friday is a want. Here's the test. Ask yourself, can I get through this month without it? If the answer is yes, it's a want.
Peter: Okay, okay. My phone is a need, right? I need it for school groups.
Stewie: A working phone is a need. The brand new phone you saw in an ad yesterday is a want. That's the sneaky part, Peter. Most wants disguise themselves as needs.
Peter: Like my new shoes. My old ones are fine, but the new ones are… cooler.
Stewie: Exactly. That's a want wearing a need costume.
Peter: But my friends all have them. I'll look like a loser.
Stewie: That's called peer pressure, and it's the most expensive thing you'll ever pay for. Here's a trick. When you want something, wait twenty-four hours. If you still want it tomorrow, and it fits your budget, buy it. Most of the time, the feeling disappears.
Peter: Wait, so Instagram ads are trying to make me buy stuff I don't need?
Stewie: Peter, that's literally their entire job.
Peter: Freakin' sweet. I mean, freakin' scary. So what do I do first?
Stewie: List your needs first. Bus pass, recharge, exam fee, books. Pay for those. Whatever is left over goes to wants. Never the other way around.
Peter: Hehehehe. Leftovers. Like biryani.
Stewie: Needs first, wants get the leftovers. Do that, and you'll never be broke by day ten again.
Reel 2: The 50-30-20 Rule
#503020Rule #BudgetingTips #SavingGoals #OpportunityCost #FinancialLiteracy #TeenMoney #MoneyBrainrot
Peter: Hey Stewie, I want a six thousand rupee cycle. I'm just gonna manifest it.
Stewie: Manifesting is not a financial strategy, Peter. A budget is. Have you heard of fifty, thirty, twenty?
Peter: Is that a cricket score? Who's batting?
Stewie: It's a way to split your money into three parts. Fifty percent goes to needs. Thirty percent goes to wants. And twenty percent goes to savings.
Peter: Okay, but I only get two thousand rupees a month.
Stewie: Perfect. Fifty percent of two thousand is one thousand rupees for needs. That's your bus pass, recharge and school stuff. Thirty percent is six hundred rupees for wants. Snacks, movies, games. And twenty percent is four hundred rupees that goes straight into savings.
Peter: So four hundred a month for a six thousand rupee cycle is… one, two, three… fifteen months? I'll be in college, Stewie!
Stewie: Then adjust. Cut two hundred rupees from your wants. Now you save six hundred a month, and you get the cycle in ten months instead of fifteen.
Peter: So every samosa I skip is cycle progress?
Stewie: Precisely. Every rupee you spend on one thing is a rupee you can't spend on something else. That's called opportunity cost.
Peter: Opportunity cost. Sounds like something rich people say on yachts.
Stewie: Rich people say it because they understand it. Now, the second secret. Make your goal specific. Not, I want a cycle someday. Say, I want a six thousand rupee cycle in ten months, so I'll save six hundred every month.
Peter: What if I get extra money? Like, from my uncle during Diwali?
Stewie: Bonus money is a cheat code. Put at least half of it towards your goal, and you'll get there months faster.
Peter: And what if I mess up one month?
Stewie: Then you adjust and keep going. A budget isn't about being perfect. It's about knowing where your money goes, instead of wondering where it went.
Peter: So a budget isn't a punishment?
Stewie: No, Peter. A budget isn't a cage. It's a plan for the things you actually want.
Hard Reel 1: The No-Cost EMI Trap
#NoCostEMI #BuyNowPayLater #EMITrap #HiddenCharges #CreditScore #FinancialLiteracy #MoneyBrainrot
Peter: Hey Stewie, I got a thirty thousand rupee phone on no-cost EMI. Just five thousand a month for six months. It's basically free money! Hehehehe.
Stewie: Peter, nobody lends you thirty thousand rupees out of the goodness of their heart.
Peter: But it says no cost. It's right there in the name, Stewie.
Stewie: Here's the trick. An EMI is a loan. The lender still charges interest. The shop gives you a discount equal to that interest, so the total price looks the same. But you usually still pay a processing fee, and GST on the interest part. So no-cost often means some cost.
Peter: Okay, that's like a few hundred rupees. Not a big deal.
Stewie: That's only the start. Now you've promised to pay five thousand every month for six months. What happens if your part-time job stops, or there's an emergency at home?
Peter: I… skip a month?
Stewie: Miss even one EMI and you pay late fees and penalty interest. Worse, it goes on your credit score.
Peter: What's a credit score? Is it like a high score?
Stewie: It's your money report card, usually a number between three hundred and nine hundred. Every loan and EMI you take, and whether you paid on time, gets recorded. Later, when you want an education loan or a credit card, banks check that number. A bad score follows you for years. Like the smell of your car.
Peter: Hey!
Stewie: And here's the real trap. Buy now pay later makes everything feel cheap. A phone EMI, shoes on pay later, a game on EMI. Each one looks small, but together they eat your whole income every month.
Peter: So what should I do?
Stewie: Before any EMI or pay later, ask three questions. What's the total I'll actually pay, including fees? What happens if I miss a payment? And can I afford this every single month, even if something goes wrong?
Peter: Or… I just save for three months and buy a cheaper phone?
Stewie: Peter, that's the smartest thing you've ever said. If you can't afford it twice, you can't afford it on EMI.
Module 2: Save & Manage (Peter & Stewie)
Reel 3: The Chai Math
#ChaiMath #SmallSpends #UPISpending #SavingHabits #Subscriptions #FinancialLiteracy #MoneyBrainrot
Peter: Hey Stewie, I'm broke again, and I didn't even buy anything big this month. I think a ghost is stealing my money.
Stewie: The ghost is you, Peter. Open your UPI history. Read it out loud.
Peter: Chai, twenty rupees. Samosa, thirty. Chai, twenty. Game top-up, forty-nine. Chai. Chai. Chips. Chai…
Stewie: Twenty rupees a day feels like nothing, doesn't it? Now do the math. Twenty rupees a day is six hundred rupees a month. And seven thousand three hundred rupees a year.
Peter: Seven thousand?! Stewie, I drank a whole phone.
Stewie: And that's just chai. Add the snacks, the game top-ups, the late night food delivery. Small amounts, every day, quietly add up to something huge.
Peter: But they're so small! How was I supposed to notice?
Stewie: That's exactly the problem. UPI makes money invisible. When you pay with cash, you see the notes leaving your hand and it hurts a little. With UPI, it's one tap and a happy little sound. Your brain doesn't even register it.
Peter: The little sound is so satisfying though.
Stewie: It's satisfying for the shopkeeper, Peter. Now check your subscriptions too. That music app, that streaming app, that game pass you forgot about. Ninety nine here, one forty nine there, cutting money every single month.
Peter: Oh no. I'm paying for three apps I haven't opened since summer.
Stewie: Cancel them. Now, here are the two habits that fix all of this. Habit one. Save first. The day money comes in, move your savings into a separate account or pot before you spend a single rupee. Spend only what's left.
Peter: Pay myself first. Like a CEO. Hehehehe.
Stewie: Habit two. Every Sunday, spend two minutes scrolling your UPI history. Just looking at where your money went makes you spend smarter the next week.
Peter: So do I have to quit chai? That's inhumane.
Stewie: No. You don't have to quit what you love. You just have to choose it on purpose. Chai three times a week, not three times a day.
Peter: Deal.
Stewie: Small leaks sink big ships, Peter. Save first, then spend what's left.
Reel 4: The Emergency Fund
#EmergencyFund #RainyDayFund #SavingsTips #StudentMoney #InstantLoanApps #FinancialLiteracy #MoneyBrainrot
Peter: Stewie! My phone screen just cracked! The repair is three thousand rupees and I have, like, forty. I'm finished.
Stewie: Let me tell you a story about two Peters.
Peter: Two of me? The world isn't ready for that.
Stewie: Peter A saved two hundred rupees every month into a separate pot that he never touches. After a year and a bit, he has about three thousand rupees sitting there. His phone breaks, he pays for the repair, fixes it the same day, done. Zero stress.
Peter: Peter A sounds cool. I like Peter A.
Stewie: Peter B saved nothing. His phone breaks, so he borrows from a friend. Then he can't pay it back on time, and it gets awkward. So he downloads an instant loan app. Huge interest, hidden charges, and when he's late, the app starts calling everyone in his contacts list. Even his mom.
Peter: Peter B is a disgrace.
Stewie: You are Peter B. That separate pot Peter A had is called an emergency fund. It's money you keep only for real surprises. A broken phone, a medical bill, a sudden fee hike at school, or helping your family when something goes wrong.
Peter: Is a new video game an emergency? It's on sale!
Stewie: No, you fat oaf. A sale is not an emergency. An emergency is something you didn't plan for and can't avoid.
Peter: Okay, but how much should I even save?
Stewie: Start small. Even one hundred rupees a month. Your first goal can be two or three thousand rupees. When you start earning a salary, the usual rule is to slowly build up enough to cover three to six months of your expenses.
Peter: And where do I keep it? Under my mattress?
Stewie: Keep it separate from your spending money, like a different savings account, so you don't spend it by accident. And if you ever use it, refill it before you buy anything fun.
Peter: Okay. Starting today, I'm Peter A.
Stewie: Good. You can't predict emergencies, but you can always be ready for them.
Hard Reel 2: The Credit Card Minimum Due Trap
#CreditCard #MinimumDue #DebtTrap #CompoundInterest #CreditScore #FinancialLiteracy #MoneyBrainrot
Peter: Hey Stewie, my credit card bill came. It says twenty thousand rupees, but look, the minimum due is only one thousand! I'll just pay that. Easy!
Stewie: Peter, you've just found the most expensive button in all of finance.
Peter: But it says minimum. That means it's the least I have to pay!
Stewie: Paying the minimum only saves you from a late fee. It does not save you from interest. The moment you don't pay the full bill, you lose your interest-free period, and the remaining balance starts getting charged interest. On many cards, that's around three to three and a half percent every single month.
Peter: Three percent? That's nothing!
Stewie: Three and a half percent a month is roughly forty percent a year, Peter.
Peter: Forty percent?! That's higher than my IQ!
Stewie: For once, you're right. Let's do an example. You have a twenty thousand rupee balance, interest of about three and a half percent a month, and you only ever pay the minimum. It would take you around six years to clear it, and you'd pay around forty-eight thousand rupees in total.
Peter: I paid for the same stuff two and a half times?!
Stewie: That's compound interest working against you. Interest gets added to your balance, and then you get charged interest on that interest. The same snowball that helps savers destroys borrowers.
Peter: Okay, but at least I can withdraw cash from the card when I'm short, right?
Stewie: Absolutely not. Cash withdrawals on credit cards usually have a fee, and interest starts from day one. No grace period at all.
Peter: This card is a villain, Stewie.
Stewie: The card isn't the villain. Bad habits are. And all of this hurts your credit score, which banks check for future loans.
Peter: So how do people use cards without getting destroyed?
Stewie: Three rules. Pay the full bill every single month. Never spend more than you already have in your account. And never use it for cash. A credit card is for convenience, not extra money. And remember, these are example numbers. Real rates vary by card.
Peter: Got it. Full bill, every month.
Stewie: Exactly. Minimum due means maximum pain.
Module 3: Understand Investing (Rick & Morty)
Reel 5: Compounding
#Compounding #StartEarly #PowerOfCompounding #InvestingBasics #FinancialLiteracy #MoneyBrainrot
Morty: R-Rick, I'll start saving when I'm older. Like twenty-eight. That's when you're supposed to have money, right?
Rick: Morty, that's the dumbest thing I've heard since Jerry's business plan. Sit down. I'm going to teach you the most powerful force in finance.
Morty: Is it a portal gun?
Rick: Better. It's called compounding. Let's start simple. Say you have one thousand rupees, and it grows ten percent a year. That's just an example number, Morty, not a promise.
Morty: Okay. So after one year, I have one thousand one hundred.
Rick: Right. Now here's the magic. In year two, you don't earn ten percent on one thousand. You earn ten percent on one thousand one hundred. So you get one hundred and ten, not one hundred. That extra ten rupees is money earned by your money's money.
Morty: Money's money? Oh geez, my brain.
Rick: Your returns start earning their own returns. That's compounding. At first it looks tiny. Then it snowballs. Now let me show you two Mortys.
Morty: Two of me?
Rick: Both put away five hundred rupees a month until they're fifty-eight. Same amount, same example growth rate of ten percent a year. One Morty starts at eighteen. The other starts at twenty-eight.
Morty: Same five hundred rupees, so they get almost the same, right?
Rick: Wrong. The Morty who started at eighteen ends up with about thirty-one lakh rupees. The Morty who started at twenty-eight ends up with about eleven lakh.
Morty: What?! The early Morty only put in sixty thousand rupees more! How is it twenty lakh more?
Rick: Because those extra ten years let the snowball keep rolling. And the biggest growth happens at the end. So the earlier you start, the longer the snowball rolls, and the bigger it gets.
Morty: So time is basically a cheat code?
Rick: The only cheat code that's legal in every dimension. Two rules, Morty. Start early, even with a tiny amount. And don't keep pulling your money out, or you break the snowball.
Morty: Got it. Start small, but start early.
Rick: And this is for learning, not investment advice. Time beats timing, Morty. Every single time.
Reel 6: Risk and Diversification
#RiskAndReturn #Diversification #DontPutAllEggsInOneBasket #InvestingBasics #FinancialLiteracy #MoneyBrainrot
Morty: Rick! My friend put all his money into one thing and doubled it in a month! I'm going all in!
Rick: And what happens when that one thing crashes, Morty? You become a cautionary tale.
Morty: It w-won't crash. It only goes up.
Rick: That's what everyone says right before it crashes. Rule one. Risk and return always come together. Safer options grow slowly and steadily. Riskier ones might grow more, but they can also fall hard, sometimes really fast. A bigger possible reward always means a bigger possible loss.
Morty: So risky stuff is bad?
Rick: Not bad. Just risky. It depends on two things. First, when do you need the money? If you need it next month for fees, it should not be anywhere risky. Money you won't need for many years can handle more ups and downs. Second, how would you feel if it dropped a lot? If you'd panic, that's too much risk for you.
Morty: Okay, but how do I protect myself?
Rick: Picture a fruit seller in the market who only sells mangoes. Then there's a bad mango season, and his income drops to zero. His neighbour sells mangoes, bananas and apples. One crop fails, and the other two keep him going.
Morty: So don't sell only mangoes.
Rick: Exactly. That's diversification. You spread your money across different kinds of things, so one bad event can't wipe you out.
Morty: But my friend just got rich from one thing!
Rick: That's survivorship bias, Morty. You only hear from the winners. The guys who lost everything aren't posting screenshots. And remember, just because something went up last month, doesn't mean it'll go up next month.
Morty: Oh geez. So what should I do?
Rick: Learn first. Understand what you're putting money into. Never put in money you need soon. And never go all in on one thing because a friend said so.
Morty: Got it.
Rick: This is for learning, not investment advice. Just remember, never put all your eggs in one basket.
Hard Reel 3: Inflation
#Inflation #RealReturn #RuleOf72 #PurchasingPower #MoneyMath #FinancialLiteracy #MoneyBrainrot
Morty: Rick, I'm keeping ten thousand rupees in a box under my bed. Zero risk!
Rick: Zero risk? Morty, there's a monster eating that money while you sleep. It's called inflation.
Morty: A monster?!
Rick: Inflation means prices go up over time. Every year, the same things cost a little more. Your grandpa's one rupee samosa costs twenty rupees now. A movie ticket that cost fifty rupees years ago costs two or three hundred today.
Morty: B-but my ten thousand is still ten thousand.
Rick: The number on the note stays the same, Morty. It just buys less. That's called losing purchasing power.
Morty: How fast is it shrinking?
Rick: Here's a genius-level shortcut. The Rule of seventy-two. Divide seventy-two by the inflation rate, and you get roughly how many years it takes for prices to double. Let's assume six percent inflation. Seventy-two divided by six is twelve. So in about twelve years, prices roughly double, and your ten thousand rupees buys only about half as much.
Morty: My money is shrinking while I sleep?!
Rick: Now think about your goals. If a college course costs one lakh rupees today, at six percent inflation it could cost around two lakh in twelve years. So when you plan a goal, you have to plan for the future price, not today's price.
Morty: Oh geez. Okay, what's the hard part?
Rick: Real return. Say your money earns four percent a year, but inflation is six percent. Four minus six is minus two. Your number goes up, but what it can buy goes down. You're getting poorer while thinking you're winning.
Morty: So the goal is to beat inflation?
Rick: Bingo. Your money needs to grow faster than prices rise. Learn how different options compare to inflation, and talk to a trusted adult or a registered advisor before you choose anything. These are example numbers, Morty, not promises.
Morty: Got it.
Rick: Remember, money that sits still is slowly losing value.
Module 4: Stay Financially Safe (Rick & Morty)
Reel 7: Never Share Your OTP
#OTPScam #UPIFraud #KYCScam #QRCodeScam #CyberSafety #ScamAlert #MoneyBrainrot
Morty: Rick, a guy from my bank just called. He said my KYC expires today and my account will be blocked in one hour. He just needs the OTP I got. Okay, it's four, eight…
Rick: Morty, stop! Don't say another number!
Morty: B-but he's from the bank! He even knew my name!
Rick: Scammers can get your name and number from data leaks, Morty. That doesn't make them your bank. Three rules. Tattoo them on your brain.
Morty: Okay, okay.
Rick: Rule one. Banks never ask for your OTP, your PIN, your CVV or your password. Not on a call, not on WhatsApp, not on SMS. Never. An OTP is like a signature. Sharing it means you're approving whatever they're doing.
Morty: Never. Got it.
Rick: Rule two. You never need your UPI PIN to receive money. If someone says enter your PIN to get your refund, or scan this QR code to receive your prize money, money is going out of your account, Morty. Not in. Scanning a QR code is for paying, not receiving.
Morty: Oh geez. My cousin almost scanned one of those while selling his old cycle online!
Rick: Classic trick. Rule three. Don't click links in random messages, and never install a screen sharing or support app because a caller asked you to. Those apps let them see your screen and control your phone, including your bank apps.
Morty: But he sounded so official, Rick. He was so polite.
Rick: They practise sounding official. Panic and urgency are their weapons. Your account will be blocked, your parcel is stuck, your SIM will stop working. All designed to make you act before you think. If you're worried, hang up, and call the number on the back of your bank card or on the bank's official website yourself. Not a number from a random search result.
Morty: And if someone already got scammed?
Rick: Call nineteen thirty right away, that's the national cyber crime helpline, or report it on cybercrime dot gov dot in. Then call your bank to block your card or account. The faster you report, the better the chance of stopping the money.
Morty: I should tell my grandparents about this too.
Rick: Finally, a smart idea. Remember, your OTP is your house key, Morty. Never hand it to anyone.
Reel 8: Spot the Scam
#SpotTheScam #ScamAlert #TooGoodToBeTrue #TaskScam #LoanAppScam #DigitalArrest #FinancialLiteracy #MoneyBrainrot
Morty: Rick! I'm gonna be rich! I got five amazing offers today!
Rick: Oh boy. Let's play real or scam, Morty. Read the first one.
Morty: Double your money in thirty days. Guaranteed.
Rick: Scam. Nobody in any dimension can guarantee high returns. Real investments go up and down. Red flag number one, guaranteed returns.
Morty: Okay, second one. Like YouTube videos and earn five thousand rupees a day. Join our Telegram group.
Rick: Scam. It's called a task scam. They pay you a little at first, so you trust them. Then they say, to unlock bigger tasks, you have to deposit money. You deposit, and they vanish. Red flag number two, easy money for no real work.
Morty: Third one. Congratulations, you won twenty-five lakh rupees! Just pay a two thousand rupee processing fee to claim it.
Rick: Scam. You can't win a lottery you never entered, and real prizes never charge you first. Red flag number three, an upfront fee.
Morty: Fourth one. Instant loan, no documents, approved in two minutes.
Rick: Big danger. Fake loan apps ask for access to your contacts and photos. Then they charge crazy interest and harass you and your family. Only borrow from lenders registered with the RBI. Red flag number four, no checks, too fast, and it wants your phone data.
Morty: Last one. A video call from a police officer! He says my name is in a crime case, and I'm under digital arrest unless I transfer money to clear it.
Rick: Scam, Morty! There's no such thing as a digital arrest. Real police don't arrest people on video calls or ask for money to close a case. Red flag number five, fear and threats. Hang up and tell an adult.
Morty: So I'm… not rich. And not arrested.
Rick: No, Morty. But you're also not scammed. In this family, that's a win.
Morty: How do I remember all this?
Rick: Simple. Urgency plus a guarantee plus pay first equals scam. Every time.
Hard Reel 4: The Money Mule
#MoneyMule #BankAccountScam #CyberCrime #DontRentYourAccount #ScamAwareness #FinancialLiteracy #MoneyBrainrot
Morty: Rick! Easiest job ever! A guy on Instagram will pay me five thousand rupees just to use my bank account for a few days. I d-don't even have to do anything!
Rick: Morty, that job has a name. Money mule.
Morty: Like… a donkey?
Rick: Worse. Here's how it works. Scammers steal money from victims. Someone's grandma who shared her OTP, someone who fell for a fake job. They can't keep that money in their own accounts, or they'd get caught. So they rent accounts from people like you, and move the stolen money through them.
Morty: B-but I didn't steal anything!
Rick: Doesn't matter. The stolen money went through your account, so the trail leads straight to you. Not to the scammer. To you. Your account can get frozen, your name ends up in a police complaint, and you can face criminal charges.
Morty: For five thousand rupees?!
Rick: Yep. And they're smart about it. They don't say be a money mule. They say part-time job, work from home, earn commission, or just help us receive some payments. They target students, because students need money and don't know the risk.
Morty: So what are the warning signs?
Rick: Anyone who asks you to lend your account, receive money and forward it to someone else, sell your SIM card or UPI ID, or share your net banking login. Or a job that needs your bank details, but no real skills or interview.
Morty: What if a friend just wants to borrow my account? Like, just once?
Rick: Same answer. Your account, your name, your responsibility. Never share your account, your debit card, your cheque book, your SIM or your login. Not even with friends.
Morty: And what if someone's already doing this and wants to stop?
Rick: Stop immediately. Tell your parents or a trusted adult, contact your bank, and report it on cybercrime dot gov dot in, or call nineteen thirty. Reporting early is always better than getting caught later.
Morty: Got it.
Rick: Remember, Morty. If they pay you to use your account, you're the one who gets caught.
